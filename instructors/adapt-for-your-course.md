# Adapt the companion files for your course

The student files in this repository are written for any course. The license lets you adapt them with attribution,
and most take a few minutes. Edit them once, post them beside your course materials, and assign them in week one.
This page follows section 9.3.5 of *Learning with AI* (Part II).

## The companion files

| File | What to put in it |
|---|---|
| [`companion/dpp/`](../companion/dpp/) | The personal-preferences template: your course, its level, its prerequisites, and its notation |
| [`companion/spp/`](../companion/spp/) | The Study Partner Protocol: the course's mentor prompt, with the emphasis your discipline needs and the course's scope. For assignments where AI may tutor, you can add the optional help ladder there |
| [`students/checklist.md`](../students/checklist.md) | Your field's checks, added to the verification checklist |
| Starter prompts | Five or six prompts for the tasks your course actually assigns |

The editing pays back. An instructor who has written the course's mentor prompt knows what every student's AI has
been told, so the threads students share are easier to read. In a course with several sections, one person keeps the
folder for all of them, so every student's AI hears the same course.

## The rules

Add the rules as well:

- your institution's AI guidelines and its list of approved tools;
- the AI section of your syllabus.

A student's AI that has the rules can help the student keep to them. Post them in order, institution, course, then
assignment, and say that the assignment's label decides when they differ. Prefer a short version of a long policy,
which an AI can misread. The student, not the AI, stays responsible for following them.

## Practice for checking answers

The folder can also hold practice for the answer-checking habits in Part I:

- a few worked solutions with a typical mistake planted in each;
- for key problems, the checks a right answer must pass, such as its expected size or a limiting case;
- the week's learning objectives and the mistakes students in your course most often make, so the AI quizzes students
  on what matters.

Plant the errors yourself. The errors you plant are the ones your students actually make; an AI asked to plant an
error in its own work may pick an easy one.
