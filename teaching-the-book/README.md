# Teaching a course with *Learning with AI*

*For instructors who use the book as the text for a course. Exercise sets cover Part I and Appendix A;
Parts II and III are to follow.*

The book was written as a guide for three readers: students, instructors, and the people who run departments
and universities. It was not written as a textbook, and it has no problem sets. This folder supplies what a
course needs: a choice of course formats, a sample schedule, and exercises for each chapter.

## Which courses fit

| Course | Audience | Chapters | Length | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Graduate seminar: learning and research with AI** | Graduate students in any STEM field | Part I, Section 7.7, Appendix A | 8 to 10 weeks, 1 credit | **Strong.** Part I and the research appendix are written for this reader. |
| **First-year seminar: learning with AI** | Entering undergraduates | Part I | 6 to 8 weeks | **Strong**, using the simpler exercises in each set. |
| **Module inside a STEM course** | Students in a course that permits AI | Chapters 1 to 4, plus one study session | 2 to 3 weeks | **Strong.** The repository already supplies most of the material. |
| **Seminar on teaching and learning with AI** | Graduate students, future faculty, teaching assistants | Parts I and II, selected Part III chapters | 10 to 14 weeks | **Good.** Students read Parts II and III from their own side of the desk. |
| **Full-semester undergraduate textbook** | Any | All | 14 weeks | **Weak.** Too little of the book speaks to one reader for a whole semester. |

## The exercises

[How the exercises are built](exercise-design.md) sets out the ten tests every exercise meets and the shape of
each chapter's set. In brief, each set has:

- a map from exercises to the chapter's key points;
- a label at the start of every exercise giving its assignment category (Section 3.4);
- short questions answered from memory, without AI;
- exercises that apply the chapter to the student's own courses;
- exercises that use AI;
- starred exercises for students who want more;
- a check one week later, without AI;
- a weak and a strong response to the main exercise, with notes on what separates them.

**Parts II and III are read from the student's side.** Their exercises do not ask students to act as
instructors or administrators. They ask students to look at how teaching, assessment, and policy shape their
own learning, and to judge what the book proposes from where they sit.

## Notes for instructors

- Pick exercises; do not assign every one. A typical week uses the memory questions, one application
  exercise, and one AI exercise.
- Grade the reasoning, not the answer. Each exercise says what the grade rests on.
- The check a week later is the only part that measures learning. Keep it short, keep it without AI, and do
  not skip it.
- If you run a course with these exercises, the author would like to hear what worked and what did not. See
  [FEEDBACK.md](../FEEDBACK.md).
